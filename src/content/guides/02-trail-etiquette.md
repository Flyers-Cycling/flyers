title: Trail manners
summary: How to share the trail with hikers, horses, and other riders, and keep it in good shape.

Our trails are shared, and the people who build them are volunteers. Good trail manners keep everyone safe and keep the trails open to bikes.

## Who goes first

- Bikes yield to hikers and horses. Stop, step to the side, and let them pass.
- Riders going downhill yield to riders climbing.
- With horses, stop and ask the rider how they'd like you to pass. Talk in a normal voice so the horse knows you're a person.

## Passing

Say hello well before you get close. "Rider back, passing on your left when you're ready" works. Wait for a safe spot.

## Stay on the trail

Ride through puddles and over rocks, not around them. Going around widens the trail and kills plants. Don't cut corners or make new lines.

## Skip muddy trails

Riding wet dirt leaves ruts that last all season. If your tires are picking up mud, the trail is too wet. This is one reason we call off rides for mud.

## Leave no trace

Pack out everything you bring, including wrappers and broken parts.

## Ride in control

Ride at a speed where you can stop for whatever is around the next corner.
